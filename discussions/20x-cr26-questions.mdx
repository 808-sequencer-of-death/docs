## Bottom line

20x is currently for cloud-native services built on a FedRAMP-certified IaaS or PaaS. Self-hosted and on-premises services can get a 20x Class A certification, then move to Rev5 Class B or higher until 20x rules for self-hosted services exist. You can get a Class A certification without an independent assessment. You can't hold both a 20x certification and a Rev5 program certification. FedRAMP doesn't prescribe secure configuration benchmarks such as STIG or CIS. You choose a defensible approach and document it.

## Class A specifics

<Note>
  **emu-gov, 2026-09-09** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-18369371)) and **pete-gov, 2026-09-09** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-18371743)): You can get a Class A certification and a Marketplace listing without an independent assessment, and two certified providers did. Staff say MAY really means MAY. Having an assessor check your work first is allowed and might go more smoothly, but it's "100% not expected." For reference, the published Class A variant of [`IVV-CSX-AIA`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/independent-verification-and-validation/#annual-independent-assessments-for-20x) says providers MUST meet the expectations of their underlying alternative security framework.
</Note>

<Note>
  **emu-gov, 2026-09-02** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-18249814), [follow-up](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-18249940)): If you hold Class A and apply for Class C, the Class C review is still your "initial certification." The note about six months of KSI metrics carries over: you need mechanisms in place and an agreement to meet the requirement. Class A certification reports include feedback for moving up to Class B or C.
</Note>

## Secure configuration

Rules page: [Secure configuration guide](/rules/secure-configuration-guide).

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-07-17** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-17674707)) and **2026-09-02** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-18255608), [follow-up](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-18256476)): FedRAMP doesn't prescribe CIS benchmarks or DISA STIGs. The CR26 Rev5 control guidance has no SA-04 guidance requiring them. For certification, there's no FedRAMP minimum tier. You pick a risk-based configuration, apply it, and document why it meets the control. Agencies may still write such requirements into contracts. Closed RFCs don't apply. Read the linked outcome, such as [NTC-0013](https://www.fedramp.gov/notices/0013/), instead.
</Note>

<Note>
  **nicole-gov, 2026-09-02** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-18258430)) and **dan-fedramp, 2026-09-03** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-18269575)): The guide required by [`SCG-CSO-RSC`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/secure-configuration-guide/#recommended-secure-configuration) tells agency customers how to configure **your service** securely, with settings specific to your offering. Leave out OS configuration unless agencies configure the OS themselves.
</Note>

## Adopting rulesets and showing compliance

<Note>
  **paulagosta, 2026-07-23** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-17755820)): Paul was answering a Rev5 provider whose grace date falls before its annual assessment.
  - Moving to a ruleset needs no third-party validation first. You just follow the rules.
  - Some rulesets require notice. For example, [`CDS-CSF-TCM`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/certification-data-sharing/#trust-center-migration) requires notice to FedRAMP at info@fedramp.gov.
  - FedRAMP asks for status through FSI and at your annual assessment.
  - Your monthly ConMon should show the change. For example, your POA&M becomes a monthly activity report.
</Note>

<Note>
  **nicole-gov, 2026-07-24** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-17766043)): Adopting new FedRAMP rules that change your processes will almost certainly be a significant change. Follow the [significant change notification](/rules/significant-change-notification) rules and notify customers.
</Note>

If reviewers have to hunt for files, FedRAMP will push back on your submission.

## Scope and Marketplace listings

Rules pages: [Minimum assessment scope](/rules/minimum-assessment-scope), [Marketplace listing](/rules/marketplace-listing), [Certification data sharing](/rules/certification-data-sharing).

<Note>
  **emu-gov, 2026-08-17** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-18053044)): Whether a service is in FedRAMP scope depends on how the agency uses it. The examples on the [Scope of FedRAMP](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/scope/#scope-of-fedramp) page aren't an exclusion list. A key test is general education and training offered to the public. FedRAMP doesn't issue blanket out-of-scope determinations.
</Note>

<Note>
  **emu-gov, 2026-09-09** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-18368851)) and **pete-gov, 2026-09-09** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-18369926)): A feature that can't be bought on its own can't have its own listing. List it as a service under the parent offering, and include its components in the [`MAS-CSO-IIR`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/minimum-assessment-scope/#identify-information-resources) scope. A standalone product with its own team, package and ConMon is a separate offering with its own listing.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-05** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-18766044)) and **2026-10-06** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-18781924)): For software installed locally in an agency, the cloud service's control over it is in scope. That includes command and control, telemetry and updates. The software itself, running in the agency environment, isn't. You can include information about it under [`MAS-CSO-SUP`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/minimum-assessment-scope/#supplemental-information).
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-09-02** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-18255553)): No rule requires a full trust center for an Initial Implementation listing. The explicit rules govern, not the narrative. The rules that apply are:
  - [`MKT-CSO-MLR`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/marketplace-listing/#marketplace-listing-requirements), which points to [`CDS-CSO-PUB`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/certification-data-sharing/#public-information): public information in human-readable and JSON formats.
  - [`MKT-CSO-PML`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/marketplace-listing/#provider-marketplace-listing-requests): the listing request form.
  - [`MKT-IIP-AGU`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/marketplace-listing/#agency-use-cases), [`MKT-IIP-DCP`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/marketplace-listing/#demonstrating-continuous-progress) and [`MKT-IIP-DLA`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/marketplace-listing/#deadline-for-assessment).

  A public FedRAMP landing page with that information counts as a "basic FedRAMP-compatible Trust Center."
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-16** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-18978806)): An initial Marketplace listing under [`MKT-IIP-AGU`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/marketplace-listing/#agency-use-cases) is fine if you intend agencies to use the offering, even with no named agency customer. Scope the offering to the parts you expect to make available to agencies that are likely to handle or affect confidentiality, integrity or availability of federal customer data. For agency data in agency-managed storage, everything handling it, and everything likely to affect its confidentiality, integrity or availability, is in play. A storage service added later can join the existing certified scope as a significant change, or be a separate certified offering if it's a different product. Staff said FedRAMP can't give architecture advice.
</Note>

## Vulnerability evaluation and reporting

Rules page: [Vulnerability evaluation and reporting](/rules/vulnerability-evaluation-and-reporting).

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-13** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/147#discussioncomment-18903585)): PAIN N0 and accepted vulnerabilities aren't linked. You may accept the risk of a vulnerability at any PAIN. Track a vulnerability until it's **remediated**. A fully mitigated vulnerability is still present, so keep tracking it. See [`VER-TFR-MAV`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-evaluation-and-reporting/#mark-accepted-vulnerabilities): you MUST categorize any vulnerability not fully mitigated or remediated within 192 days of evaluation as accepted.
</Note>

## What it means for you

- If your service isn't cloud-native on a certified IaaS or PaaS, Class A is your 20x entry point today. Moving higher means Rev5 Class B or above until rules for self-hosted services exist.
- For Class A, an independent assessment is optional. Check the Class A rules listed in [`FRC-CLA-MFR`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/fedramp-certification/#mandatory-fedramp-rules-for-class-a).
- Write your secure configuration guide for agency customers configuring your service, not as an OS hardening benchmark.
- Treat rule adoption as a significant change and notify customers.
- In your SDR, use machine-readable answers with direct artifact links, not pointers to legacy documents.
