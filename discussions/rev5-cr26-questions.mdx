## Bottom line

FedRAMP staff treat the Consolidated Rules for 2026 (CR26) as mandatory for every Rev5 provider on the dates in the [Rev5 deadlines table](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/providers/updating/deadlines/rev5/). If you miss a "maintaining" date, expect a corrective action plan and an **In Remediation** status. If you miss the grace date, expect revocation. Vulnerability detection and response (`VDR`) and vulnerability evaluation and reporting (`VER`) come first, on 2026-12-07. When you adopt them, POA&Ms give way to VER reports, and staff expect you to handle the switch as a significant change. FedRAMP stops accepting new Rev5 certifications, including uplifts, on 2027-06-11.

## Deadlines, grace periods and enforcement

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-06-25** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-17437758)): Each ruleset has its own timeline at the top of the ruleset. The VDR and VER rulesets carry the NTC-0014 dates: 2026-12-07 to obtain or maintain certification, and a grace period ending 2027-03-07. This matches current upstream ([VDR](/rules/vulnerability-detection-and-response), [VER](/rules/vulnerability-evaluation-and-reporting)).
</Note>

<Note>
  **emu-gov, 2026-07-09** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-17587600)): The "obtaining" dates apply to new submissions and class changes, such as an uplift. Anything submitted on or after 2027-01-01 must conform to the new rules or risk denial.
</Note>

<Note>
  **emu-gov, 2026-07-10** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-17596382)): [Addressing FedRAMP communication](/rules/addressing-fedramp-communication) (`AFC`) and the [secure configuration guide](/rules/secure-configuration-guide) (`SCG`) are already required. Their grace period ended 2026-07-01. Optional early adoption of CR26 started 2026-07-04. Packages submitted before 2027-01-01 may mix legacy Rev5 and CR26. The provider, assessor and agency agree on the mix, keeping the obtaining dates in mind.
</Note>

<Note>
  **emu-gov, 2026-07-16** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-17661653)): The rules have fixed dates. FedRAMP will try to be reasonable about real extenuating circumstances, but don't count on leniency if you haven't started on VDR and VER.
</Note>

<Note>
  **emu-gov, 2026-07-30** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-17845287)): If a Rev5 Class C provider doesn't implement CR26, the consequence is a corrective action plan, then revocation of FedRAMP Certification, on the dates in the [important deadlines](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/providers/updating/deadlines/#important-deadlines).
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-28** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-19250459)): Asked whether a Ready Conversion assessor may use a penetration test from a third party that isn't a FedRAMP-recognized assessor, staff said CR26 doesn't require a penetration test from a FedRAMP-recognized independent assessor. You can include another assessor's results in your Vulnerability Detection and Response activity reports. If you submit under the legacy rules and convert to CR26 by February, staff said they will accept a POA&M or finding for lacking a recognized penetration test. You would document what you did instead and why you see no significant resulting risk, and the finding goes away after you switch. The answer doesn't speak directly to whether the assessor may review and incorporate the results, and the rules don't address the legacy-rules arrangement.
</Note>

<Note>

Staff said it will be obvious fairly quickly if providers aren't supplying them. All the significant change rules apply, and many significant changes don't need independent assessment.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-21** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-19108724)): For an active Rev5 provider, 2027-08-01 is when a service goes into remediation and needs corrective action under the trust center (`CDS`) rules, though staff strongly discourage waiting. The ruleset's own grace date is 2028-02-01. See [Certification data sharing](/rules/certification-data-sharing).

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-22** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-19131028)): FedRAMP will confirm VDR implementation by asking you and reviewing your required VDR and VER activity reports. It won't evaluate progress along the way. Full implementation is expected by 2026-12-07, and if you miss it you supply a corrective action plan to finish by 2027-03-07. Staff warned that the grace period covers FedRAMP Certification only, and that agencies must report on BOD 26-04 compliance by 2026-12-07.
</Note>

<Note>
  **paulagosta, 2026-08-06** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-17920098)): The VDR and VER dates follow the 180-day timeline in CISA BOD 26-04. If you miss a maintaining date, expect a corrective action plan with a fix date no later than the grace date, and a "deficient" flag on the Marketplace. After the grace period, expect removal. "First FedRAMP independent assessment" means initial and annual assessments, not significant change assessments. The grace date depends on when the assessment **starts**, not when it's due. For example, if your annual assessments start in November, rules with an "assessment started after 2027-01-01" grace date apply at your November 2027 assessment.
</Note>

On the Rev5 side, only the `CDS` ruleset requires you to tell FedRAMP when you transition. For the five rulesets whose grace period is tied to the assessment start date, FedRAMP will ask for your planned timeline around the maintaining date.
</Note>

## Scope of CR26 for Rev5, packages and certification paths

<Note>
  **emu-gov, 2026-08-20** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18093065)): A Rev5 certification needs only the rules with Rev5 applicability. All 15 rulesets apply to both Rev5 and 20x. The big difference is controls (Rev5) versus Key Security Indicators (20x). Each subset's applicability (type, path, class, audience) tells you which rules are in scope, and some rules also vary by class.
</Note>

<Note>
  **paulagosta, 2026-08-24** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18138185)): For Lost Sponsor and Ready Conversion providers, assessment results now go in the [certification package overview](/rules/certification-package-overview) (`CPO`) and [security decision record](/rules/security-decision-record) (`SDR`). `IVV-IAS-SUM` says no separate SAP or SAR is required.
</Note>

<Note>
  **paulagosta, 2026-08-24** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18138144)): FedRAMP won't accept a Rev5 uplift or net-new certification after 2027-06-11, whenever you entered the agency pipeline. `pete-gov` said the same on 2026-07-31 ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-17855511)): you must have applied by 2027-06-11, and anything still In Process after that date can't finish. See also the [timeline](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/timeline/).
</Note>

<Note>
  **paulagosta, 2026-08-06** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-17920337)): This answered a question about a Rev5 Class D service that isn't cloud-native and has no 20x path yet. Rev5 certifications remain as long as you meet your FedRAMP obligations and transition to the CR26 rulesets. Other services can still leverage the offering. Your listing stays active unless you stop meeting your obligations, ask to leave, or FedRAMP retires Rev5. An RFC on 20x Class D was pending at the time. It was later published as [RFC-0033](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/170) (see [RFC history](/discussions/rfc-history)).
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-07** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18797658)): FedRAMP has no DISA reciprocity path. DISA or your agency ATO holder can sponsor a Rev5 agency certification, or you can build a 20x package and apply for program certification.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-08** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18817999)): All Li-SaaS authorizations are grandfathered into Rev5 Class B for now. The plan is for them to move to 20x Class B or full Rev5 Class B, and an RFC is expected. Until then, continue with legacy Li-SaaS. Migration timelines will give at least a year of runway. CR26 lets new certifications reuse recent assessment results and focus on what changed.
</Note>

## Vulnerability detection and response (VDR)

Rules page: [Vulnerability detection and response](/rules/vulnerability-detection-and-response).

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-09-13** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18425215)): [`VDR-TFR-NMV`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-detection-and-response/#non-machine-verification-and-validation) means you verify and validate documentation, personnel and budgets every 3 months. Verify means collect evidence that they're followed. Validate means check that the evidence shows they're followed correctly. That's separate from the review and update cadence for policies under the Rev5 "-1" controls. Tracking last-verified dates in your [minimum assessment scope](/rules/minimum-assessment-scope) inventory makes sense, and you summarize this work in your VDR activity reports.
</Note>

<Note>
  **dan-fedramp, 2026-08-14** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18021674)): [`VDR-CSO-SIR`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-detection-and-response/#sampling) lets you monitor a sample of identical resources, such as containers from the same image, instead of every instance. Sampling is always optional. [`VDR-TFR-PSD`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-detection-and-response/#persistent-sample-detection) sets the detection cadence, which you can meet by scanning everything or by scanning samples.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-09-08** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18356896)): Under [`VDR-TFR-PVR`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-detection-and-response/#mitigation-and-remediation-expectations), a successful mitigation leads to a new evaluation, and the clock resets from that evaluation. Example: if you evaluate a vulnerability on 9/2 and lower its PAIN on 9/18, the new deadline is 9/18 plus the timeframe for the new rating. The goal is to mitigate first. The timeframes are worst cases.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-08** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18817958)): FedRAMP doesn't prescribe how to apply [`VDR-CSO-DET`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-detection-and-response/#vulnerability-detection) to software composition analysis, code scans or SBOMs. You choose the approach, then document and share it. Staff strongly recommend grouping at scale to reduce noise.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-13** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18909538)), on existing vulnerabilities at transition: VDR deadlines run from evaluation, not detection. You must report the gap between detection and evaluation and start evaluating promptly. A longer gap during the transition is fair. Start evaluating your backlog now, prioritize what's serious, then report.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-14** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18923577)): This answered a question from `nathan-gov` about long-standing POA&M milestones agreed with agencies. Most can move over and be tracked as accepted vulnerabilities. No FedRAMP rule requires agency concurrence to accept vulnerabilities after initial certification, but your contracts might.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-20** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-19073144)) and **2026-10-22** ([follow-up](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-19131580)): Detection time is when the detection system found the vulnerability, not when a later system processed it. The `VDR-TFR-PVR` timelines run from evaluation. Whether IaC-managed resources are unlikely to drift is for your engineering team to decide. If a vendor fix isn't available, mitigate instead of only accepting the vulnerability, and explain the risk and impact to customers if you don't. A fully mitigated KEV still needs remediation, and the staff comment gave 14 days. [`VDR-TFR-KEV`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-detection-and-response/#remediate-kevs) is a SHOULD that follows the CISA catalog due dates and doesn't state 14 days, so the published rule governs. In the follow-up, staff said the SHOULD can rest on valid technical reasons you can't compensate for, but agencies may not accept them.
</Note>

## Incident communications

<Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-28** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-19250378)): A community member pointed out that the `IEC-CSO-IIR`, `IEC-CSO-OIR` and `IEC-CSO-FIR` rule statements say to notify "FedRAMP and directly impacted federal customer agencies," while the notification requirement also lists "all necessary parties" through the trust center. Staff called that an oversight, because these rules don't require notifying all necessary parties. They opened [FedRAMP/Rules#47](https://github.com/FedRAMP/rules/issues/47) and said they will make that notification optional. Posting incident information in the trust center remains helpful but is up to you. Staff also said an incident notification must not enable a threat actor, so sanitize it as appropriate. In most cases posting incident reports publicly should be fine. In rare cases where even the existence of an incident could create a threat, share it carefully with affected parties only. This is a stated plan, and the upstream text hasn't changed yet, so the published rule governs until FedRAMP updates it. It refines the 2026-10-21 note above.
</Note>

## Vulnerability evaluation and reporting (VER)

Rules page: [Vulnerability evaluation and reporting](/rules/vulnerability-evaluation-and-reporting).

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-28** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-19249797)): Asked whether using only exploitability, reachability and PAIN would fall short of VER, staff said the [`VER-EVA-EFA`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-evaluation-and-reporting/#evaluation-factors) factors help you do the evaluations the other rules require. It isn't either-or, and you can't accurately judge likely exploitability under [`VER-EVA-ELX`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-evaluation-and-reporting/#evaluate-exploitability) without considering the relevant factors. Staff said PAIN isn't a ninth factor: reachability and exploitability are inputs, while "internet reachable," "likely exploitable" and PAIN are outcomes. Staff also pointed to the "It's Not a Trap" section of the [rules overview](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/rules/#its-not-a-trap). For SHOULD rules, do what your engineers support, be ready to explain why it's technically right, and FedRAMP isn't going to nitpick. The published `VER-EVA-EFA` is a SHOULD.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-09-10** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18391276)) and **2026-09-11** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18402431)): Under [`VER-EVA-EPA`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-evaluation-and-reporting/#estimate-potential-agency-impact), PAIN is the cumulative effect on the federal government. A disruptive effect on more than one agency is N4. Derive it from your inventory with a rules engine where you can. If you have only one agency customer, N5 can't apply. With no agency customers there's no PAIN, but staff expect serious providers to simulate it. Rating everything as if it affects multiple agencies is acceptable and conservative.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-09-28** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18644936)): A vulnerability doesn't simply inherit its component's PAIN. Start from the worst case, then lower the rating for the vulnerability's actual technical impact. Staff "STRONGLY" encourage rating below the component's ceiling when the effect is narrower. Don't lower PAIN for reachability or exploitability. Those are separate evaluations under [`VER-EVA-ELX`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-evaluation-and-reporting/#evaluate-exploitability) and [`VER-EVA-EIR`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-evaluation-and-reporting/#evaluate-internet-reachability).
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-02** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18714893)): Once [`VER-EVA-EFP`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-evaluation-and-reporting/#evaluate-false-positives) finds a false positive, the other evaluations are done: exploitability, reachability and PAIN are all null. You can report them as not applicable. One option is to report false positives as accepted vulnerabilities in a "false positive" group under [`VER-EVA-GRV`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-evaluation-and-reporting/#group-vulnerabilities). He said he'd clarify `VER-EVA-EPA` to allow PAIN 0 or N/A.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-15** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18957963)): The CVSS user interaction metric doesn't by itself show that a vulnerability isn't automatable under [`VER-EVA-AIA`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-evaluation-and-reporting/#assume-its-automatable), but you can use it as evidence. A flaw that needs a specific action on one terminal you've walled off is probably not automatable. A flaw that only needs someone to open an email attachment still is, because "someone is gonna open that email." In general, high user interaction requirements are likely to reduce automatability.
</Note>

<Note>
  **paulagosta and pete-gov, 2026-09-11** ([Paul](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18401490), [Pete](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18402346), [Pete follow-up](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18403756)), on agencies that still ask for a legacy POA&M or SSP:
  - Agencies may add needs beyond FedRAMP through their contracts.

<Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-13** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18903643), [follow-up](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18904451)): [`IVV-IAS-SUM`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/independent-verification-and-validation/#assessment-summary) is the assessor's summary for each FedRAMP Practice.   **pete-gov, 2026-10-15** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18957818)): How the assessor reports on [`IVV-IAS-VIP`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/independent-verification-and-validation/#verify-inclusion-in-certification-package) and gets access to the final package is for the provider and assessor to settle in their contract. ## Certification data sharing and trust centers

Rules page: [Certification data sharing](/rules/certification-data-sharing).

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-07-31** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-17852670)): The trust center rules, including [`CDS-TRC-USH`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/certification-data-sharing/#uninterrupted-sharing), are deliberately minimal. If agencies routinely tell FedRAMP they can't get the access they need, FedRAMP is likely to conclude the rules aren't met. Staff prefer a high-quality customer experience over minimal compliance.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-07** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18799636)): [`CDS-CSO-PUB`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/certification-data-sharing/#public-information) makes providers own their public information. FedRAMP imports it into the Marketplace, and agencies can pull it straight from you.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-21** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-19108724)): A third-party trust center vendor doesn't need a FedRAMP certification, at Class D or any class. The Minimum Assessment Scope doesn't require FedRAMP Certified third-party resources, but you must address their potential impact on federal customer data. If you manage package information responsibly, the trust center could sit outside the scope entirely.
</Note>

## JSON schemas

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-06-27** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-17450997)) and **2026-10-02** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-18716792)): CR26 schemas are deliberately minimal. Whether the schema marks a field optional or required says nothing about whether the rules require that information. Follow the written rules, not templates or schemas. For example, the `SCN` schema doesn't list every item in [`SCN-CSO-INF`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/significant-change-notification/#required-information), but the rule still applies.
</Note>

## What it means for you

- Plan to meet every applicable VDR and VER rule by 2026-12-07. Staff say the VER [Reporting](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-evaluation-and-reporting/#reporting) outputs are your evidence until you update your SDR.
- Handle the VDR and VER transition through your significant change process (`SCN` or legacy SCR). Record where your new reports live.
- Check your own dates against the [Rev5 deadlines](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/providers/updating/deadlines/rev5/). For `FRC`, `IVV`, `MAS`, `CPO` and `SDR`, the grace period ends at the first independent assessment **started** after the listed date.
- If you plan a Rev5 uplift or new Rev5 certification, it must reach FedRAMP before 2027-06-11.
- For `SDR-CSO-FRR` and `SDR-CSF-CTF`, give both your method and its results, and follow the rule text rather than the schema.
