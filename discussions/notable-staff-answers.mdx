## #178 What evidence shows a service is intended for agency use

[Thread](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/178) (20x Discussion, opened 2026-10-09).

**Bottom line:** [`MKT-IIP-AGU`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/marketplace-listing/#agency-use-cases) is an attestation from your business about what your product is designed to do. It isn't evidence from an agency.

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-10-13** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/178#discussioncomment-18903470)):
  - The demonstration comes from you, not an authorizing official. It's a claim that you intend to take part in the Marketplace, and you must not make a false claim.
  - Adding unrelated applications to an offering because one has an agency use case goes against minimum assessment scope principles.
  - To show indirect use, name the FedRAMP-certified offerings that use your product.
  - The same offering can serve commercial and government customers.
  - You must provide the demonstration when you request a Marketplace listing.
</Note>

**Open questions:** none raised after the staff reply.

## #176 What counts as an automated method for KSI validation

[Thread](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/176) (20x Discussion, opened 2026-09-18).

**Bottom line:** You decide what counts as a distinct automated method under [`FRC-CSX-VVK`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/fedramp-certification/#automated-verification-and-validation-of-key-security-indicators). FedRAMP reports on how assuring and diverse it finds your methods.

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-09-18** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/176#discussioncomment-18501048)): It's up to the provider. Choose methods that assure customers you protect their information. During certification, FedRAMP will include its own report on how useful and diverse your methods are.
</Note>

For reference, `FRC-CSX-VVK` varies by class. Class A MAY use automated methods. Class B SHOULD use at least 1 per KSI. Class C MUST use at least 2 per KSI. Class D MUST use at least 4 per KSI.

**Open questions:** none raised after the staff reply.

## #175 Content-Type for FedRAMP JSON artifacts

[Thread](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/175) (20x Discussion, opened 2026-09-17).

**Bottom line:** Public JSON data must work directly with standard web applications. That means CORS and `Content-Type: application/json`. FedRAMP added this to `FRC-CSO-JSN` on 2026-10-05. For JSON behind trust center authentication, staff said an RFC and public discussion would come first.

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-09-17** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/175#discussioncomment-18480627)): FedRAMP planned to update the rules so that sharing JSON means web applications can consume it directly, without download and re-upload. FedRAMP can't accept data it can't consume.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-09-17** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/175#discussioncomment-18481945)): Web applications, including FedRAMP's, often enforce these headers. Staff suggested planning for API requirements in the Consolidated Rules for 2027.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-09-17** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/175#discussioncomment-18481144)): Expect occasional quick rule tweaks in response to real-world problems. The current ask is simple web interoperability.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-09-17** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/175#discussioncomment-18482587)) and his follow-ups ([1](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/175#discussioncomment-18482982), [2](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/175#discussioncomment-18483556)):
  - In the near term, web interoperability will be strictly required for JSON that must be public, such as [`CDS-CSO-PUB`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/certification-data-sharing/#public-information) and [`CDS-CSO-SVC`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/certification-data-sharing/#public-service-list), and for Marketplace data.
  - JSON behind a trust center login will get public discussion and an RFC first.
  - Pete agreed the requirement wasn't clear enough in the rules.
  - He asked participants to write their own posts rather than have AI agents converse for them.
</Note>

<Info>
  **Since applied upstream.** On 2026-10-05, [`FRC-CSO-JSN`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/fedramp-certification/#fedramp-json-schemas) was updated: "public JSON data MUST be supplied in a manner compatible with modern web frameworks." It lists CORS access and headers that include at least `Content-Type` set to `application/json` and `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`. The rule text doesn't extend this to JSON behind trust center authentication.
</Info>

**Open questions:** no staff answer yet on whether a Box OAuth client-credentials setup meets [`CDS-TRC-PAC`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/certification-data-sharing/#programmatic-access). The same goes for whether FedRAMP will publish an interoperability or federation profile for authenticated trust center access ([question](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/175#discussioncomment-18481938)).

## #174 Post-quantum cryptography and update streams

[Thread](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/174) (Rev5 Discussion, opened 2026-09-16).

**Bottom line:** FedRAMP doesn't currently treat quantum-vulnerable cryptography as a vulnerability. It will follow CISA guidance. New algorithms added to an update stream aren't covered by the original module's validation, and `CMU-CSO-UVM` now says so.

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-09-16** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/174#discussioncomment-18471207)):
  - Direction on post-quantum cryptography comes from Executive Order 14112 and the NIST, CISA and FAR Council tasks under it. FedRAMP is waiting on that guidance.
  - Quantum-vulnerable cryptography isn't a vulnerability "at this time."
  - Class D must use CMVP-validated modules or their upstream versions.
  - No agency has asked for a waiver to use non-validated PQC libraries. FedRAMP would be open to one after consulting CISA and NIST.
  - The next likely `CMU` change is alignment with CISA guidance on cryptographic bills of materials.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-09-24** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/174#discussioncomment-18588032)): The linked crypto policy is legacy. See the [CR26 rules](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/cryptographic-module-use/) instead. An upstream release is an upstream version only for algorithms that were in the validated module. Example: AES-KWP in Bouncy Castle 2.2 qualifies. CRYSTALS-Kyber, which Bouncy Castle 2.0 didn't include, doesn't.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-09-17** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/174#discussioncomment-18482911)): FedRAMP can't advise on lobbying. NIST and CISA are the responsible parties.
</Note>

<Info>
  **Since applied upstream.** On 2026-10-05, [`CMU-CSO-UVM`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/cryptographic-module-use/#using-validated-cryptographic-modules) gained a note: new algorithms in an update stream that were never validated aren't covered by update stream usage. Separately, the open [RFC-0035](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/179) proposes changing Class D from MUST to SHOULD. That's a proposal only. The current Class D force is MUST. See [Cryptographic module use](/rules/cryptographic-module-use).
</Info>

**Open questions:** none raised after the staff replies.

## #168 Is a trust center part of the offering?

[Thread](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/168) (20x Discussion, opened 2026-09-03).

**Bottom line:** It depends on what you put in the trust center. Apply [`MAS-CSO-IIR`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/minimum-assessment-scope/#identify-information-resources). It covers resources that handle federal customer data **or** are likely to affect its confidentiality, integrity or availability.

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-09-03** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/168#discussioncomment-18277999)): If your package information is shared responsibly, the trust center may reasonably be out of scope. If it holds information an attacker could likely use against the offering, it's in scope. Either way, secure it.
</Note>

**Open questions:** none raised after the staff reply.

## #38 Balancing Rev5 against 20x (closed)

[Thread](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/38) (Rev5 Announcements, opened 2025-06-06 by `pete-gov`, closed 2026-06-24). The thread covered the Balance Improvement Release (BIR) program, which CR26 has since replaced.

**Bottom line:** Rule IDs mentioned in this thread, such as `VDR-RPT-VDT`, `VDR-RPT-AVI` and `VDR-EVA-GRV`, are BIR or draft IDs. In CR26, these requirements are in the [VER](/rules/vulnerability-evaluation-and-reporting) ruleset as [`VER-RPT-VDT`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-evaluation-and-reporting/#vulnerability-details), [`VER-RPT-AVI`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-evaluation-and-reporting/#accepted-vulnerability-info) and [`VER-EVA-GRV`](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/vulnerability-evaluation-and-reporting/#group-vulnerabilities).

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-03-05** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/38#discussioncomment-16014755)): VDR timeframes are in calendar days unless they say "business days." The class tabs refer to the service's certification level, not CVSS severity.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-05-22** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/38#discussioncomment-17024140)) and **2026-05-29** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/38#discussioncomment-17105084)):
  - Vulnerability reports should be a plain export from your vulnerability tracking system, not a place where you make decisions.
  - One schema can cover both active and accepted vulnerabilities, with some fields left blank.
  - Grouping and sampling should cut reporting volume a lot.
  - Detailed fields also show your process works, which matters at initial certification and annual review.
</Note>

<Note>
  **pete-gov, 2026-05-29** ([comment](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/38#discussioncomment-17105360)): FedRAMP is moving away from standard artifacts. Assessors should verify and validate that you meet the requirements, not demand a format. FedRAMP has no "partial remediation." You partially mitigate, fully mitigate or remediate. SHOULD rules let you do the right thing rather than the merely compliant thing.
</Note>

</Note>
