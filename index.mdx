This site explains the [FedRAMP Consolidated Rules for 2026](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/) in plain English. It also tracks what FedRAMP staff say about those rules in the [FedRAMP community discussions](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions).

## Who it's for

This site is for teams at cloud service providers (CSPs) that hold, or are working toward, a FedRAMP Certification.

- **Executives** get the bottom line first: what each ruleset makes you do, by when, and how strict it is.
- **Engineers and compliance leads** get the detail: every rule ID, its force (MUST, SHOULD or MAY), class differences, timeframes and the deliverables you have to produce.

<Warning>
  **These summaries aren't the rules.** The official text at [fedramp.gov/2026](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/) governs. FedRAMP's machine-readable source of truth is the [FedRAMP Rules repository](https://github.com/FedRAMP/rules). Every summary here links to the official rule it describes, so check the source before you act on anything.
</Warning>

## How this site stays current

- **Nightly sync.** Every night, automation pulls the latest [FedRAMP/2026-markdown](https://github.com/FedRAMP/2026-markdown) release (the Markdown edition of the official rules) and the latest posts from the [FedRAMP community discussions](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions).
- **Human review.** Every update to this site is reviewed by a person before it's published. Nothing goes live automatically.
- **Staff versus community.** Discussion digests quote only FedRAMP staff posts as FedRAMP positions. Community comments are context, not guidance. A staff comment never overrides the published rule.
- **Release tracking.** Each rule summary names the upstream release it was checked against. The current release is `2026.10.14.01`. See [What changed](/changelog) for the history.
