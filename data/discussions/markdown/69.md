## dan-fedramp (FedRAMP) — [2025-08-01T18:11:53Z](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/69#discussioncomment-13961885)

When you're sending in your 20x package, remember to reference the Key Security Indicator (KSI) using its exact id from the official KSI documentation [here](https://github.com/FedRAMP/docs/blob/main/FRMR.KSI.key-security-indicators.json). Each element in the JSON document has a unique identifier in the 'id' field. It makes things a lot simpler if you use the full id as written.  For example, KSI-CED-01 is spot on, but steer clear of formats like KSI-CED-1 or ksi-ced-01 - those won't match due to the case sensitivity and need for a leading zero.

I'd suggest CSPs check their submissions against the most recent KSI JSON file as part of their automated system. This helps in keeping up with any KSI changes and helps us review packages quicker and smoother.

The submissions we've seen so far have impressed us with their focus on security and efficiency. While there haven't been any major hiccups, nailing these small details like getting the KSI ids right will help us grow the 20x program and support future efforts in automation.

## emu-gov (FedRAMP) — [2025-08-01T18:34:30Z](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/69#discussioncomment-13962007)

The KSI family `KSI-RPL` is for **Recovery Planning** which is an area near and dear to my heart as someone who has spent much of my career elbows deep in outage and/or incident response.

Some excellent examples of what we have received include not only a Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery Plan or Service Level Agreement where targets for RPO & RTO are initially established, but also an automated calculation of the RPO based on actual database and storage backup configuration and a boolean output of if it maths out.

RPO seems to be a commonly misinterpreted metric. [Defined by NIST](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/recovery_point_objective) as _"The point in time to which data must be recovered after an outage"_ - an alternate way to view is the maximum amount (in time) of acceptable loss of customer and/or service data.

If your data backup frequency (24hrs) is greater than your RPO target (8hrs), you are probably not going to consistently meet that target...

## kyhu65867 — [2025-08-04T20:17:56Z](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/69#discussioncomment-13993332)

A little late to the party here, but we have been very excited to see scripts and script-generated output provided as evidence, as it is very much in the spirit of 20x automated assessments!

That being said, to facilitate our review process, we recommend the following: If you are using custom scripts, you should include the script code with the output. If the output is generated by a tool from a PaaS/IaaS/SaaS provider or another third party, you should provide the name of the tool and the provider as metadata with the evidence. Providing this information greatly assists us in understanding and assessing the results effectively :)

## dan-fedramp (FedRAMP) — [2025-08-08T17:18:24Z](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/69#discussioncomment-14049002)

Piggybacking off of [kyhu65867’s](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/69#discussioncomment-13993332) post, it's a good idea to include some context with each piece of KSI evidence. At a minimum you should include the following elements:

Provide an explanation of how the evidence satisfies the KSI and what the criteria is for the Pass, Fail or Partial decision.
Include information about where the KSI applies specifically for the cloud service offering (scope).
When referencing a document, provide either excerpts or specific locations (section numbers for example) that show the KSI is met
For each artifact, indicate if it was collected manually or automatically.  Remember one of the goals for the 20x program is to automate as much of the security assessment as possible.
When providing evidence that a policy or procedure exists also provide evidence that the process is being executed. If no evidence of execution is available, indicate that.   

This context will really help anyone understand how the KSI is achieved and the connection between the KSI implementation and the KSI evidence. It is also important to remember that an agency or FedRAMP reviewer may not be familiar with the CSP offering or the specific services being used from the cloud hosting provider. So if you are making use of specific cloud services it is helpful to include a short explanation of what the service does and/or links to additional information.

## emu-gov (FedRAMP) — [2025-08-15T17:57:20Z](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/69#discussioncomment-14119611)

We've been getting lots of questions about where the submission package should be located 👀 

For the 20x-P1 Pilot, we are not leveraging connect.gov or a FedRAMP hosted centralized location. The expectation is that the submission be hosted by the CSP and access provided to the FedRAMP review team.

Ideally this would also be the Trust Center or central shared location as described in [RFC-0011](https://www.fedramp.gov/rfcs/0011) which can then be used to grant access to future agency customers, but this is a pilot and we have tried to be as flexible as possible.

So far we have accessed submissions via file shares on Trust Repositories, directly connected to a GRC Audit Tool or CSP hosted application, or via private Git repositories. 

We support public facing submissions as much as possible as it gives the community a way to learn, contribute, and iterate from one another ❤️ - but we absolutely understand that certain KSI validation information and evidence may be proprietary or non-public.

## nicole-gov (FedRAMP) — [2025-08-29T18:52:09Z](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/69#discussioncomment-14258821)

Thanks so much for everyone's patience as we chip away at the overwhelming response to the Phase 1 Pilot! As always, an influx of packages generates new feedback, so stay tuned for our general thoughts as we work to close out Phase 1 and look forward to Phase 2 later this fall. 

In the theme of sneak peeks for Phase 2 requirements, this week's focus is automated validation and supporting evidence. The intent of FedRAMP 20x is to move toward continuous real-time evaluation of a CSP's security posture. Manual evidence collection doesn't quite meet the bar that we're setting. Some of the really cool pilot efforts we've seen have solved this with real-time trust centers and/or APIs that report on the current state of the service, but those certainly aren't the only ways to solve this problem. For Phase 2, you can expect a remarkably heightened threshold for automated validation and evidence (in other words, fewer screenshots please!). 

In a similar vein, as you're preparing your submission, independent assessors should be reviewing BOTH your evidence AND your automated validation that produced it (is it complete, is it accurately polling the cloud service offering's configuration, is it automated or human-generated, does it fully meet the KSI, etc.). Your independent assessor should be validating that the evidence accurately demonstrates a true/false/partial response to the KSI and also that the method that produced that evidence is a valid and complete approach for your particular cloud service offering. Also, don't be surprised if we here at FedRAMP also want to see your automated validation as we work through evaluating this pilot effort together! 

## emu-gov (FedRAMP) — [2025-09-19T20:51:26Z](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/69#discussioncomment-14460954)

We heard you loud and clear on KSI-IAM-05 during the Phase One pilot - that it is nebulous, that zero trust principals don't just apply to Identity and Access Management, and that it was generally confusing.

We also saw this KSI being used as almost a rollup of the other IAM KSI validations. What was *Apply zero trust design principles* is now **Design identity and access management systems that assume resources will be compromised**.

Some examples of the thought process for this KSI are below and this update is included in [RFC-014](https://www.fedramp.gov/rfcs/0014) which is currently open for feedback!

- What protections are in place to mitigate a hijacked JWT? 
- Are your SSO and trust relationships well configured? 
- Are all resources explicitly locked down or are insecure direct object reference vulnerabilities possible?
- What about permission propagation and inheritance? 

The ask for this KSI is to think about ways your IdP, trust chain and any services that manage access to information resources could be compromised and how based on your specific architecture, movement within your environment would be limited.

We also have `3` other Request for Comments active 🙀 - checkout our [RFC Page](https://www.fedramp.gov/rfcs).
