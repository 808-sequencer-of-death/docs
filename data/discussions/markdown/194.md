## pete-gov (FedRAMP) — [2026-11-09T16:30:52Z](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/194#discussioncomment-19496803)

> How will the FedRAMP PMO ensure that agencies are using 20X?

The U.S. federal government is a massively complex system of bureaucracy including hundreds of agencies and thousands of authorizing officials who are all responsible for creating their own policies based on statute and regulation. FedRAMP has no authority or responsibility to "ensure" that agencies use FedRAMP Certified cloud services or leverage the FedRAMP process; the extent of FedRAMP's responsibility for agency use is to supply that process and support it with templates, best practices, technical assistance, and other materials. 

Oversight of agencies and their adherence to statute and regulation is provided by entities that are specifically designed for that. At a board level, this includes:

- Congress, including via the Government Accountability Office, who monitors agency compliance with regulations and frequently enforces them.
- The Executive Office of the President, especially via the Office of Management and Budget, who also monitors agency compliance (especially with OMB directives, such as OMB Memorandum M-24-15) and frequently uses the President's budget process to enforce priorities.
- Inspectors General, who audit larger agencies on a frequent basis to ensure they are acting efficiently and effectively in compliance with the law and policy.

All of these entities have in the past and likely will continue to provide oversight that includes monitoring how effectively agencies use FedRAMP Certified cloud services.

**In reality,** what dictates agency use is agency need. When agencies need a cloud service, they will find a way to use it. It will be considerably easier for them to use it if it has a FedRAMP Certification, especially a FedRAMP 20x Certification. But FedRAMP doesn't create that demand - cloud services do, by providing a service that is critical enough to agency operations for the agency to want to use it.

> **pete-gov (FedRAMP)** — [2026-11-10T19:48:59Z](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/194#discussioncomment-19527613)
>
> > ... unlike in the past, FedRAMP is no longer receiving or tracking those ATO letters, and so usage was likely more prevalent than the marketplace would indicate.
> 
> There's a lot that goes into this - the [FedRAMP Authorization Act](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/authority/law/agencies/#c-submission-of-authorizations-to-operate-required) requires agencies to tell us when they ATO a FedRAMP Certified cloud service, and when that act passed and OMB was doing a ton of engagement with agencies about FedRAMP in the lead up to M-24-15, we had a HUGE spike in notifications. Lots of agencies were shipping us literal buckets full of old ATO letters so they didn't get yelled at by OMB or their IG. 
> 
> Afterwards though, the same thing happened that always happens... lots of agencies never established a whole workflow or policy to do this and only use a couple new cloud services a year (if that), so when they ATO'd a new cloud service 9 months later they didn't send us the letter. Then last year there were pretty big changes in staffing and we have to train up more folks across agencies and they don't always want to send us letters, plus in general agency usage of new cloud services has dropped dramatically.
> 
> Result is the current situation - we have a few stalwart agencies that let us know, a few more that tell us back-channel, and a lot of folks that simply don't let us know at all and we find out about usage from the cloud service provider. 
> 
> It **is** required by the law. Again though, FedRAMP has no enforcement authority or responsibility. All we can do is keep reminding folks. We expect a GAO audit might catch this, and some agency IG audits would catch it, but they'll all be coming out over the next year+ and agencies might not pay attention. Is what it is.
> 
> Anyone looking at 20x usage should also look at Rev5 usage. You'll see for example that something like 2/3 of the cloud services that got a Rev5 in 2025 have seen no reuse according to Marketplace data, and that around a third of all-time Rev5 services have no reuse at all.
> 
> Looking at reuse data for 20x certifications that are weeks old is silly.
