If you're following our [public roadmap](https://github.com/FedRAMP/roadmap/) you'll know that we're transitioning from 20x Phase One to Phase Two right now... and you'll know we have an _aggressive_ schedule in front of us once again.

Expect updates, policies, RFCs, criteria, content, and other details to start drop continuously this month (it's going to feel like June all over again). We are going to try to release towards the middle of the week where possible but we have some aggressive timelines to hit and that means we'll publish late in the week when we need to.

I'm going to try to keep this thread updated with information, plans, links, updates, etc. as we go. Hope you're ready for the ride!

### [FedRAMP Phase Two will be formally announced on September 24 at 3:30pm.](https://www.fedramp.gov/20x/phase-two)

Participants in Phase Two will be eligible to receive a 12 month FedRAMP 20x Moderate authorization, however, _participation will be limited; Phase Two will not be entirely open to the public._ We currently anticipate opening up formal 20x Low and Moderate authorizations, reviewed directly by FedRAMP (no agency sponsor required), in February 2026.

To get the ball rolling, I'm releasing the following summary of changes from RFC-0012 that will be released in the upcoming Vulnerability Detection and Response Standard tomorrow.

# 20x Vulnerability Detection and Response Standard Changes

The purpose of FedRAMP, as defined in modern law and policy, is not to simply to determine if cloud services are sufficiently secure for government use; FedRAMP exists to provide a standardized and reusable approach to security assessment and authorization that enables agencies to make informed risk-based decisions when integrating cloud services into agency information systems. Cloud service providers who wish to sell their services to agencies must report on the ongoing security decisions they make and agencies will review that information to determine if they wish to use these cloud services.

An early version of this standard was released as RFC-0012 Continuous Vulnerability Management (CVM) Standard, with aspects of the problems related to Plans of Action & Milestones discussed with the Federal Secure Cloud Advisory Committee. FedRAMP received a significant amount of both formal and informal public comment on the proposed CVM and has made considerable changes to this Vulnerability Detection and Response (VDR) Standard as a result.

The VDR Standard includes the following significant changes for initial release:

- Credibly Exploitable Vulnerabilities have been renamed to Likely Exploitable Vulnerabilities to reinforce the importance of assessing how likely something is, to reuse the term likely that is becoming a critical aspect of FedRAMP assessment, and to default to simpler plain language. As a bonus, the spoken acronym for LEV is clearly distinct from KEV. 
- The historical concept of Plans of Action & Milestones (POA&Ms) has been completely eliminated and replaced with simply labeling a weakness that the cloud service does not plan to fix as an accepted weakness. Agencies are expected to integrate these into their formal Plans of Action & Milestones for any federal information systems that rely on the cloud service offering based on their own policies.
- Providers are more strictly urged to avoid sharing sensitive information that would likely lead to exploitation and are no longer required to even report specifics of the underlying vulnerability itself (and agencies are more strictly urged not to request it). Instead, providers should share sufficient information so that agencies can understand the risk to their information. In practice, this should lead to less noise shared with agencies and allow them to focus on real underlying risks, but it is a significant shift for both providers and agency customers from today’s default.
- FedRAMP has defined impact effects based on commercial service impact that a provider can understand without having knowledge of the agency use cases for their service. The potential adverse impact calculation has been changed to consider impact to the entire population (N) of agency customers. Both changes enable providers to create more quantitative assessments of the overall potential adverse impact that applies for FedRAMP.
- Maximum recommended timeframes for many aspects of vulnerability detection and response have been separated by FedRAMP authorization impact level to focus the most aggressive expectations on the highest impact services.
- This policy now encourages representative sampling of resources on a more aggressive schedule than polling all resources, urges providers to automatically assess newly deployed resources, and focuses recurring assessments on resources that are most likely to change. 
- Leveraging authority in the FedRAMP Authorization Act and M-24-15, this updated policy includes agency specific requirements and recommendations.
- Many other minor changes have been made to order, wording, recommendations, and other items based on feedback. For example, many commenters were confused about partial mitigation vs full mitigation so this update clarifies that partial mitigation is encouraged in steps until full mitigation is achieved. Another example is the definition and use of “persistently” to mitigate quite a lot of confusion around the different uses of “continuous” in different contexts.

This standard will be revised and reopened for public comment based on feedback from the FedRAMP 20x Pilot Phase Two/Three and the Rev5 VDR Closed Beta; the next revision is anticipated in Jan/Feb, 2026.
