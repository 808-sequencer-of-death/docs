# Practitioner Framework for Safety-Relevance Interpretation in SBOM/VEX-Driven FedRAMP Risk Decisions
Source: https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/142

## Original post — devashridatta-dotcom, 2026-06-05T02:31:14Z

As federal mandates increasingly require SBOM submissions from Cloud Service Providers, a persistent operational gap has emerged: there is no standardized, repeatable logic for translating SBOM and VEX data into defensible risk-acceptance decisions that satisfy FedRAMP audit baselines.
Authorizing Officials and CSPs can now see vulnerabilities at scale. The harder problem is adjudicating them, determining whether a given finding warrants immediate remediation, POA&M deferral, or formal risk acceptance — in a way that is consistent, auditable, and not solely dependent on CVSS scores.
What I've been working on
I developed a Safety Relevance Interpretation Framework, a lightweight model that addresses a specific gap in current VEX-based workflows: the absence of a structured method for evaluating whether a vulnerability has functional or safety significance within a federal deployment context.
The framework evaluates three factors in combination:

Safety and Functional Relevance: Does the vulnerability touch federal data boundaries, system integrity functions, or safety-critical execution paths?
VEX Applicability and Exploitability Context: Is it technically exploitable in the actual runtime environment, given component reachability and deployment configuration?
Lifecycle Stage: Is the affected component in active deployment, legacy isolation, or end-of-life status?

These inputs feed a structured decision logic that produces one of four outcomes: immediate remediation, monitored deferral, formal risk acceptance, or escalation, each with a documented justification trail.
The intent is to give AOs and CSPs a repeatable, audit-ready interpretation layer on top of raw SBOM/VEX output, rather than leaving each team to construct ad hoc logic under time pressure.
Inviting feedback
I'm sharing this here to get practitioner and community input, particularly from those working on continuous monitoring workflows, POA&M processes, or SBOM ingestion pipelines. Specifically interested in:

Whether safety relevance as a first-order factor reflects how AOs currently evaluate findings
Where the logic breaks down at scale or across heterogeneous component inventories
Any alignment gaps with existing FedRAMP Rev 5 or OSCAL guidance

Happy to share more details on the decision logic in the follow-up discussion.
Devashri Datta, Independent Researcher, Software Supply Chain Security

Thanks Pete really appreciate you pointing me to the Vulnerability Detection and Response ruleset.
I completely understand and agree with you, the intention to keep the guidance outcome-focused and allow CSPs flexibility in how they operationalize it based on their environment and business context.
What I’m exploring here isn’t meant to prescribe a single implementation approach, but rather to make some of the implicit decision logic around vulnerability relevance more explicit and auditable, especially in the context of SBOM/VEX-driven workflows.
In practice, it seems like CSPs are already making nuanced determinations (e.g., exploitability in context, deployment exposure, mission impact), but those decisions can be inconsistently represented or difficult to trace during assessment.
My goal is to see whether a lightweight, structured interpretation layer could help standardize that reasoning without constraining how CSPs implement their underlying processes.
Would definitely welcome any perspective on how much variability you’re seeing today in how CSPs justify vulnerability relevance or risk acceptance decisions within the current ruleset.
