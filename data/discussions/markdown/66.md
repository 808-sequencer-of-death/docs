# Partnerships
Source: https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/66

## Original post — DrSC-IA, 2025-07-29T16:29:40Z

Does the FedRAMP PMO consider a 3PAO using a GRC tool to offer its assessment services the same as being a partner with that vendor?  In the assessment guidance, it states that when a 3PAO partners with a vendor who has a GRC tool, that the 3PAO cannot perform assessments for the CSP that uses that tool.  What does the PMO consider as a partnership?  I understand it to be a partnership when the 3PAO sends the vendor clients and the vendor sends the 3PAO clients and where they partner together to provide a complete service.  But if the 3PAO is using a GRC tool that they purchased from the vendor to provide a service to its client without engaging the vendor to procure these clients, then that is not a partnership.  Please clarify.  

## jsantore-cgc — [2025-07-29T19:28:07Z](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/66#discussioncomment-13926363)

When I was a 3PAO not that long ago, the way we interpreted it was this way:
I cannot make you use some specific automated assessment tool.  If I require the use of that tool, and or make you purchase it, or otherwise have a business relationship with that tool, that violates independence and therefore we could not assess.  Particularly agent based tools, or something they must host in boundary.  I could not say 'if you happen to use GRC tool X' I will give you a 10% discount, either.  Anything that even has the appearance of pushing a particular solution is not appropriate.

That's NOT the same thing as 'upload your evidence to this respository/tool' because in that case, the 3PAO would bear the cost of the tool (which again, cannot be deployed within the CSP boundary).  And even there there, there were quirks about the assessment tools themselves.  While they didn't specifically need to be FedRAMP Authorized, we couldn't just use some random thing either.  

I will say that over the years, many GRC tools tried to partner up with us, and in all cases, I could not find a way to make that work withouth violating independence.  Even if we purchased the tool, their licensing model was often such that they would to charge us to use the tool on behalf of a CSP, it was always $X per project which we could not pass along to the customer.  Also, it didn't really speed up or add efficiency to the assessment itself, either.

> **iteuscher** — [2025-08-04T18:33:38Z](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/66#discussioncomment-13992359)
>
> I believe they are talking about Section 5.2.4 F.3 of the R311 which is the [Specific Requirements: Federal Risk and Authorization
> Management Program (FedRAMP)](https://a2la.qualtraxcloud.com/ShowDocument.aspx?ID=5621) from the A2LA. Essentially the rules for 3PAOs doing FedRAMP audits. Here is the relevant section:
> 
> > 5.2.4 F.3 Tools owned or developed by a 3PAO that provide any type of direct service or support to a
> > CSP including, but not limited to, creating FedRAMP documentation are considered a form
> > of consulting. Therefore, to maintain the independence of an assessment, 3PAOs are not
> > permitted to perform assessment services for the same CSP that has directly utilized tool(s)
> > that are owned or developed by the 3PAO for any purpose. In scenarios where a tool being
> > used is owned or developed by a company with an affiliation to a 3PAO or its management
> > team, employees, or subcontractors, the 3PAO must document the rationale for how it
> > maintains its impartiality from the CSP when the CSP is using the tool owned or developed
> > by the entity affiliated with the 3PAO and how it does not infringe upon the boundaries
> > between providing consulting and assessing services to the CSP. This rationale must be
> > approved by both A2LA and the FedRAMP PMO.
> 
>

> @DrSC-IA the assessment guidance that @iteuscher referenced doesn’t say “ when a 3PAO partners with a vendor who has a GRC tool, that the 3PAO cannot perform assessments for the CSP that uses that tool.”
> 
> It states that a 3PAO can’t assess a CSP that is also using tools owned or developed by said 3PAO that provide direct services to the CSP to help them with FedRAMP.
> 
> It says 3PAOs can assess CSPs that use a tool owned or developed by a third party that provides assistance with FedRAMP that said 3PAO is affiliated with as long as they document their rationale for why it doesn’t infringe upon assessment and consulting boundaries and obtains approval of the rationale from A2LA and FedRAMP.
> 
> GRC tools providing FedRAMP readiness services are going to have business relationships with 3PAOs where they refer clients to each other, there is no question about that. I definitely think this guidance needs to be visited and updated to clearly document what is or isn’t allowed.
> 
> I am curious what the PMO’s current position is regarding the 3PAOs that performed FedRAMP assessments for GRC software providers that provide FedRAMP readiness services when the 3PAO has a business relationship with those GRC software providers. If 3PAOs are getting client referrals from the GRC software provider they assessed, it is pretty difficult to say that a conflict of interest doesn’t exist.
