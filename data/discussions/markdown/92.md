## Summary & Motivation

FedRAMP recently sent an emergency notification to the security contact addresses for all cloud service providers as part of the government-wide response to [CISA’s Emergency Directive 25-03](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/directives/ed-25-03-identify-and-mitigate-potential-compromise-cisco-devices) and discovered a systemic problem within the FedRAMP marketplace: a significant number of FedRAMP Authorized cloud services have failed to maintain their security contact information, have gated contact behind customer portals with required registration, or have otherwise cut themselves off from direct emergency communication from FedRAMP.

This proposed standard outlines clear requirements for cloud service providers to maintain a path for open communication from FedRAMP and government customers, especially during an emergency. It also explicitly outlines the punitive actions FedRAMP will take in the future against providers who do block critical communications and plans for FedRAMP to regularly assess the effectiveness of all provider’s ability to receive communications from FedRAMP.

Once formalized, providers will have a limited time to adopt this policy and should expect an initial FedRAMP-wide assessment of compliance by the end of FY26 Q2.

**Background & Authority**  
[OMB Memorandum M-24-15 on Modernizing FedRAMP](https://www.fedramp.gov/assets/resources/documents/FedRAMP_Policy_Memo.pdf) section VII (a) (17) states that GSA must *“position FedRAMP as a central point of contact to the commercial cloud sector for Government-wide communications or requests for risk management information concerning commercial cloud providers used by Federal agencies.”*

## Purpose

FedRAMP and agency customers must have a reliable way to directly contact senior security and public sector staff operating all FedRAMP Authorized cloud service offerings without maintaining separate accounts, login procedures, or other processes for individual cloud service providers. These requirements for a FedRAMP Security Inbox apply to all cloud service providers to ensure that this direct reliable path remains open.

Once finalized, FedRAMP will perform quarterly assessments of all FedRAMP Authorized cloud service offerings by sending test messages to the FedRAMP Security Inbox on file requesting a simple human response. Cloud service offerings that fail to meet these requirements (during quarterly tests or other communications) will be **immediately suspended from the FedRAMP Marketplace for a minimum of 30 days and publicly placed on a Corrective Action Plan.**

To recover from this Corrective Action Plan and return to full service on the FedRAMP Marketplace, the providers for affected cloud services will need to demonstrate they have resolved the gap in meeting these requirements. Cloud service offerings that are unable to address such a Corrective Action Plan within 30 days may lose their FedRAMP authorization entirely.

## Expected Outcomes

* Cloud service providers will prioritize review and response of emergency time-sensitive messages from FedRAMP or other agency customers.  
* Federal agencies will have a reliable way to contact security teams at cloud service providers.

# **Definitions**

**FRD-FSI-01**

**FedRAMP Security Inbox (FSI):** An email address that meets the requirements outlined in the FedRAMP Security Inbox requirements.

# **Requirements**

## FRR-FSI

These requirements apply ALWAYS to ALL FedRAMP Authorized cloud services based on the current Effective Date(s) and Overall Applicability of this standard.

**FRR-FSI-01**

Providers MUST establish and maintain an email address to receive email regarding urgent security notifications from *all necessary parties*; this inbox is a *FedRAMP Security Inbox (FSI)*. The *FedRAMP Security Inbox* is listed in the FedRAMP Marketplace as the “Security E-mail.” 

**FRR-FSI-02**

Providers MUST persistently ensure their *FedRAMP Security Inbox* receives email messages from .gov or.mil emails without any disruption, including meeting ALL of the following requirements when receiving an email originating from a .gov or .mil email address:

1. Messages MUST be routed to at least one senior security official who has authority to respond on behalf of the organization  
2. Messages MUST NOT require manual verification from the sender, logins to a web-based system, or any other sort of authentication by the sender  
3. Messages MUST NOT be automatically resolved, closed, rejected, or otherwise addressed without human review

**FRR-FSI-03**

Providers MUST follow the requirements and recommendations outlined in FRR-FSI-TF regarding timeframes for acknowledging and addressing messages received in their *FedRAMP Security Inbox.*

**FRR-FSI-04**

Providers MUST immediately notify FedRAMP of any changes in addressing for their *FedRAMP Security Inbox* by emailing [info@fedramp.gov](mailto:info@fedramp.gov) with the name and FedRAMP ID of the cloud service offering and the updated email address.

## FRR-FSI-TF

This section provides guidance on timeframes that apply to all impact levels of FedRAMP authorization for activities required or recommended in this standard; these timeframes are thresholds that secure providers should consistently strive to exceed by significant margins.

**FRR-FSI-TF-01**

Providers SHOULD immediately and automatically acknowledge the receipt of messages received from *all necessary parties* in their *FedRAMP Security Inbox*.

## FRR-FSI-TF-LO

This section provides guidance on timeframes that apply specifically to FedRAMP Low authorizations for activities required or recommended in this standard; these timeframes are thresholds that secure providers should consistently strive to exceed by significant margins.

**FRR-FSI-TF-LO-01**

Providers MUST have a human acknowledge and address messages sent by FedRAMP (including any @[fedramp.gov](http://fedramp.gov) address) to their *FedRAMP Security Inbox* within 3 business days.

**FRR-FSI-TF-LO-02**

Providers MUST have a human acknowledge and address messages sent by *all necessary parties* (other than FedRAMP) to their *FedRAMP Security Inbox* within 5 business days.

## FRR-FSI-TF-MO

This section provides guidance on timeframes that apply specifically to FedRAMP Moderate authorizations for activities required or recommended in this standard; these timeframes are thresholds that secure providers should consistently strive to exceed by significant margins.

**FRR-FSI-TF-MO-01**

Providers MUST have a human acknowledge and address messages sent by FedRAMP (including any @[fedramp.gov](http://fedramp.gov) email address) to their *FedRAMP Security Inbox* within 1 business day.

**FRR-FSI-TF-MO-02**

Providers MUST have a human acknowledge and address messages sent by *all necessary parties* (other than FedRAMP) to their *FedRAMP Security Inbox* within 3 business days.

## FRR-FSI-TF-HI

This section provides guidance on timeframes that apply specifically to FedRAMP High authorizations for activities required or recommended in this standard; these timeframes are thresholds that secure providers should consistently strive to exceed by significant margins.

**FRR-FSI-TF-HI-01**

Providers MUST have a human acknowledge and address messages sent by FedRAMP (including any @[fedramp.gov](http://fedramp.gov) email address) to their *FedRAMP Security Inbox* within 12 hours.

**FRR-FSI-TF-HI-02**

Providers MUST have a human acknowledge and address messages sent by *all necessary parties* (other than FedRAMP) to their *FedRAMP Security Inbox* within 1 business day.

## vedigaurav — [2025-10-21T14:12:58Z](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/92#discussioncomment-14741041)

Please consider below Gaps and recommendation.

Gap 1: Lack of Defined Escalation Protocols
Relevant Requirements: FRR-FSI-02: Messages must be routed to a senior security official, FRR-FSI-TF-*: Require human acknowledgment within strict timeframes
Issue: The RFC assumes a senior official will always be available to respond. But in reality, availability varies due to PTO, turnover, or off-hours.
Recommendation: Add a requirement that CSPs must define escalation paths and backup contacts to ensure coverage (especially for 12-hour and 1-day SLAs). Include language around escalation continuity plans to meet response timelines.

Gap 2: No Requirement for Inbox Monitoring/Auditing
Relevant Requirements: FRR-FSI-03: Requires timely response, FRR-FSI-TF-01: Suggests immediate acknowledgment
Issue: There is no mention of monitoring, tracking, or auditing to ensure emails are received, triaged, or escalated effectively.
Recommendation: Add a requirement for monitoring and logging access to the inbox, including:
Message receipt timestamp
Timestamp of acknowledgment and response
Responsible responder
This supports auditability and enforcement of SLAs.

Gap 3: Risk of Email Spoofing and Lack of Anti-Spam Guidance
Relevant Requirements: FRR-FSI-02: Must accept emails from .gov/.mil reliably
Issue: The RFC doesn't address security protections to prevent spoofed or malicious emails appearing to come from .gov/.mil addresses.
Recommendation: Add a control for email authentication (e.g., SPF, DKIM, DMARC) and optionally a .gov/.mil allowlist to prevent spoofing, phishing, and denial-of-service attacks via the FSI.

Gap 4: No Explicit Uptime or Redundancy Requirement
Relevant Requirements: FRR-FSI-01: Inbox must be maintained, FRR-FSI-02: Must receive email “without disruption”
Issue: "Without disruption" is vague. There is no specific requirement for email availability uptime, redundancy, or failover mechanisms.
Recommendation: Clarify that providers must implement high-availability (HA) configurations or email continuity mechanisms (e.g., fallback MX records, cloud email monitoring) to minimize downtime.

Gap 5: Ambiguity Around "Address" vs. "Acknowledge"
Relevant Requirements: FRR-FSI-TF-HI-01 to LO-02: Require human to "acknowledge and address"
Issue: "Address" and "acknowledge" are not clearly defined. Does a reply like “We’re looking into it” count as full compliance?
Recommendation: FedRAMP should define: "Acknowledge" = confirm receipt by a human, "Address" = begin action or triage with intent to resolve
This will eliminate confusion and improve SLA enforcement consistency.

Gap 6: Overloaded Use of a Single Security Email Address
Relevant Requirements: FRR-FSI-01: Single inbox listed in Marketplace, FRR-FSI-TF-HI/LO/MO: Apply to all types of messages
Issue: A single inbox may become cluttered with both routine and urgent messages, especially in large orgs with multiple FedRAMP offerings.
Recommendation: Permit CSPs to implement email aliases or internal routing rules behind the FSI (e.g., high-priority tagged messages sent to on-call staff) — as long as all routing and response requirements are met.

Gap 7: No Requirement for Email Testing or Health Monitoring by Providers
Relevant Requirements: FRR-FSI-01/02: Must always be reachable
Issue: While FedRAMP will conduct quarterly tests, there’s no requirement for providers to self-monitor or self-test inbox functionality.
Recommendation: Encourage or require CSPs to implement automated test emails or synthetic monitoring to ensure FSI functionality between FedRAMP’s quarterly tests.

The following public comment was received via email from Crowdstrike:

REQUEST FOR COMMENT RESPONSERFC-0018 FEDRAMP SECURITY INBOX REQUIREMENTS. October 3, 2025

 

INTRODUCTION

In response to FedRAMP’s request for comment on the FedRAMP Security Inbox Requirements (“draft standard”), CrowdStrike offers the following views.  We approach these questions from the standpoint of a leading international, US-headquartered, cloud-native cybersecurity provider that defends globally distributed enterprises from globally distributed threats. CrowdStrike offers insights informed by multiple practice areas: cyber threat intelligence; proactive hunting, incident response and managed security services; and an AI-powered software-as-a-service cybersecurity platform and marketplace. Accordingly, this perspective is informed by CrowdStrike’s role in protecting organizations from data breaches and a variety of other cyber threats.

COMMENTS

CrowdStrike understands the importance of timely responses to FedRAMP inquiries, and that a quick response is necessary in times of emergency. We appreciate FedRAMP's efforts to address the critical communication gaps identified during the response to CISA Emergency Directive 25-03. The request for comment correctly notes that many providers have inadvertently created barriers to emergency communications through outdated contact information and gated access systems. This creates unacceptable risks during security incidents that require immediate coordination and response. We welcome the opportunity to offer feedback that may be of value to FedRAMP as it considers the draft standard.

 

Operational Implementation Challenges

The draft standard's response time requirements, particularly the 12-hour timeframe for High authorization providers such as CrowdStrike, create significant operational burdens that require dedicated weekend and holiday staffing by senior personnel. This necessitates a fundamental shift from business-hour operations to a 24/7/365 model that many organizations have not structured or budgeted to support. The requirement for senior officials with "authority to respond" to monitor communications creates bottlenecks at the executive level rather than enabling rapid response through established security teams, potentially slowing response times during critical incidents when senior leadership should focus on strategic decision-making.

 

Business Continuity

The immediate 30-day marketplace suspension for non-compliance represents a disproportionately severe penalty that could significantly impact revenue streams and damage customer relationships. The potential escalation to complete loss of FedRAMP authorization creates an existential threat to cloud service providers' government business over what might be communication process failures rather than actual security deficiencies. These penalties do not account for legitimate technical failures or provide graduated enforcement mechanisms. The draft standard creates severe penalties without proportionate consideration of technical failures or good faith compliance efforts. Ideally, enforcement would include graduated penalties that account for the nature and cause of communication failures. Conversely, the current framework does not distinguish between willful non-compliance and technical issues beyond provider control. The standard should include a dispute resolution process that defines appeal procedures, creates clear timelines for resolution, and provides mechanisms for addressing technical failures.

 

Regulatory Ambiguity & Scope Concerns

FedRAMP should reconsider the "all necessary parties" construct within the draft standard. As written, this definition is unclear and may lead to system abuse or overwhelming security teams with non-critical requests. The distinction between "acknowledging" and "addressing" messages remains ambiguous, potentially creating compliance uncertainty.

 

Specifically:

·         'All necessary parties' should be explicitly defined to include only FedRAMP, CISA, and designated federal agency security contacts to prevent abuse.

·         'Address messages' should clearly distinguish between acknowledgement requirements and full resolution expectations.

·         Emergency versus routine communications should have clear categorization criteria to ensure appropriate resource allocation.

 

The objective of these proposed clarifications is to maintain focus on legitimate emergency communications while providing sufficient clarity for compliance.

 

CONCLUSION

We believe reliable emergency communication capabilities are essential for effective cybersecurity coordination. As the Standard moves forward and evolves, we recommend continued engagement with stakeholders to balance emergency preparedness with operational feasibility. Finally, because emergency response scenarios vary significantly, we recommend that any final framework establish realistic timeframes that account for business operations while maintaining emergency responsiveness, such as business-hour focused requirements or tiered emergency classifications that distinguish between true emergencies requiring immediate response and urgent communications that can be addressed within standard business timeframes.

## cb-axon — [2025-11-06T21:17:58Z](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/92#discussioncomment-14896019)

I think this RFC may be better served to require 2 contact points. The vast majority of email communications to email addresses given to FedRAMP and the marketplace are administrative. Much of the heartburn over 12 hour response times is because these emails are going to administrative teams, not operations centers. If we separate administrative from operationally important communications, we'll likely get the desired outcomes easily. For example, a compliance team that "manages" FedRAMP for the organization is not going to have an on-call rotation to be able to manage these officially on the weekends. But an operations center will. So BODs on the weekends will be better served by an operations center for those responses.
