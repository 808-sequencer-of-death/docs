# Rev5 questions and discussion on the Consolidated Rules for 2026 (or Legacy)
Source: https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146

## Original post — pete-gov (FedRAMP), 2026-06-24T21:49:44Z

## This thread is for any and all Q&A or general discussion related to FedRAMP Rev5 in general that does not need to have its own thread.

FedRAMP released the [Consolidated Rules for 2026](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/) on June 24, 2026 with a considerable set of changes for FedRAMP Rev5 that will apply to all cloud service providers.

These changes include:

- Mandatory adoption of the Vulnerability Detection and Response and Vulnerability Evaluation and Reporting rules by December 6, 2026 in response to CISA BOD 26-04
- Changes to assessments for Rev5 to align more like assessments for 20x - focusing on verification and validation instead of just control implementation.
- Replacement of most FedRAMP templates with a new Certification Package Overview and Security Decision Record
- Removal of many FedRAMP-assigned control parameters and a lot of older FedRAMP control guidance
- Mandatory adoption of many other Balance Improvement Releases through 2027
- A requirement to address all FedRAMP Rules within the Security Decision Record, instead of only addressing controls

Some of these changes take effect for all cloud service providers on certain dates no matter what, others take effect at the next annual assessment. In general, these are sweeping changes that will require all cloud service providers to begin planning now to continue to meet FedRAMP Rev5 requirements.

Historical materials are available at [https://fedramp.gov/legacy](https://fedramp.gov/legacy).

> **pete-gov (FedRAMP)** — [2026-07-31T10:37:56Z](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/146#discussioncomment-17852670)
>
> In general the trust center requirements are designed to allow a cloud service provider to be thoughtful and intentional about sharing information with customers while focusing on the best customer experience to help them retain and satisfy their customers. That's why they're relatively minimal. 
> 
> If agencies are routinely telling FedRAMP that they don't have the access they need to a cloud service provider's trust center, then we'd be likely to conclude that the rules aren't being met. And sometimes agencies reach out to us to complain about how shoddy someone's trust center is, and in those situations we're likely to consider other actions in the long run (such as public marketplace listings that identify the minimal compliance and access problems so agencies are aware in advance).

Historically, there may be a bit of flexibility in a CISA BOD deadline but we have seen circumstances where within a few months of such a deadline agencies have been forced to turn off services that do not comply. The grace period that FedRAMP provided is **only** for FedRAMP Certification itself, it is entirely possible that failure to comply with the VDR/VER will result in agencies turning off your service on that date and considering you in breach of contract (depending on how that's written).
> 
> We wrote VDR and VER in a way that would allow any mildly competent cloud service provider to rapidly switch over to it while gaining all of the benefits of the risk-based approach to vulnerability management, at a potential negative cost of needing to massively accelerate response to extremely rare circumstances (like this week's php2shell) _which any secure cloud service was already doing._
