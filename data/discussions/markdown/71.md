## Summary & Motivation

[FedRAMP does not plan to provide updated technical assistance](https://www.fedramp.gov/20x/faqs/) or guidance for implementations of the Rev5 baselines but [balancing Rev5 against improvements to FedRAMP 20x](https://github.com/fedRAMP/community/discussions/38) remains important. SC-7 Boundary Protection requirements are currently imbalanced across these approaches: FedRAMP 20x Key Security Indicators encourage logical separation of network resources to prevent unwanted network traversal while Rev5 baselines limit network separation to Layer 3 subnetting as defined in IETF RFC-950.

To ensure consistency in a modern approach to boundary protection between FedRAMP 20x and Rev5, FedRAMP will begin an urgent Balance Improvement Release for Rev5 as follows:

1. On August 8, 2025, FedRAMP rescinded the “[Subnets White Paper](https://www.fedramp.gov/assets/resources/documents/FedRAMP_subnets_white_paper.pdf)” to begin bringing Rev5 into balance with 20x by removing past guidance that prevented cloud service providers from using other technical capabilities that meet control SC-7. 

2. FedRAMP will formalize updated SC-7 Boundary Protection guidance in all FedRAMP baselines to clarify how this control should be met for Rev5 authorizations. That proposed updated guidance is the primary content of this RFC.

3. When formalized after public comment, this Balance Improvement Release will ***immediately*** apply to all Rev5 baselines and authorizations. There will not be a separate beta period for this release. Stakeholders can expect this change to the Rev5 baselines to occur within 30 days of completing the public comment period.

# **NIST SC-7 Boundary Protection Control Statement**

The NIST SP 800-53 Rev5 contains the following text for the SC-7 Boundary Protection control:

a. Monitor and control communications at the external managed interfaces to the system and at key internal managed interfaces within the system;

b. Implement subnetworks for publicly accessible system components that are [Selection: physically; logically] separated from internal organizational networks; and

c. Connect to external networks or systems only through managed interfaces consisting of boundary protection devices arranged in accordance with an organizational security and privacy architecture.

# **FedRAMP SC-7 Boundary Protection Control Expectations**

The following updates will apply to all FedRAMP baselines under SC-7 Boundary Protection (current guidance or procedures will be entirely replaced with the updated guidance or procedures):

## SC-7 Control Requirement(s)

### Current FedRAMP Guidance:

**SC-7 (b) Additional FedRAMP Requirements and Guidance:** SC-7 (b) should be met by subnet isolation.  A subnetwork (subnet) is a physically or logically segmented section of a larger network defined at TCP/IP Layer 3, to both minimize traffic and, important for a FedRAMP Authorization, add a crucial layer of network isolation. Subnets are distinct from VLANs (Layer 2), security groups, and VPCs and are specifically required to satisfy SC-7 part b and other controls. See the FedRAMP Subnets White Paper (https://www.fedramp.gov/assets/resources/documents/FedRAMP_subnets_whitepaper.pdf) for additional information.

### Updated FedRAMP Guidance:

**SC-7 (b) Additional FedRAMP Requirements and Guidance:** SC-7 (b) may be met by using any technical capability that ensures logical separation between publicly accessible components and internal networks by preventing traversal without inspection and authorization; traffic may not flow unrestricted from publicly accessible components to internal networks.

## SC-7 Assessment Plan/Procedures

### Current Assessment Procedure:

Determine if: 

* subnetworks for publicly accessible system components are physically; logically separated from internal organizational networks; and

### Updated Assessment Procedure: 

Determine if: 

* publicly accessible system components are logically separated from internal organizational network devices; and

SC-7(b) applies equally to customer access as well as administrator access. Since the discussion seems to be centering on admin access, I have limited my comments to this use case. While focused on the constraints of SC-7(b), by necessity these comments incorporate elements of several other control parts and enhancements. Tracking down the specific controls is left as an exercise for someone getting paid to do this. ;)

The following refers to this diagram.
<img width="1227" height="491" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/a941caa7-2729-4cda-872f-2925cf35faf8" />

**Admin Endpoint**
- Generally intended to be considered outside the authorization boundary

**Network Device(s)**
- Not generally generating content-specific responses to the Admin Endpoint
- Therefore do not meet the intent of a publicly accessible component for the purposes of the protections in SC-7(b)

**Admin Access Point**
- First authentication of Admin within the boundary
- May or may not have public IP address(es)
- Requirements
     - Inside an authorized boundary
     - Audit all access traffic
     - Traffic from administrators to the access point is authenticated at the appropriate AAL
     - Implements authentication and authorization separate from any interface being accessed
          - That is, it is only an access point
          - The same device should not host any other functionality

**Logical Separation**
- SC-7(b) calls for a physical or logical subnetwork separation
- We will allow a deviation from that here on the presumption that:
     - Subnet separation is intended to direct network traffic to a device where access controls can and will be applied
     - Software defined networking (SDN) in most IaaS/PaaS environments now supports access control application at any and all interfaces 
- Requirements:
     - Deny all; permit by exception access rules 
          - Applied to all interfaces on the access point
                    - Presumes application to each interface on the access point in an SDN environment
                    - A CSO built in a non-SDN environment (such as a co-lo facility) should hew closer to the original NIST requirement of subnetting with equivalent demonstrated access controls
          - At a minimum applied to address and port
          - Preferably also applied at the protocol level
          - Sufficient SDN example:
               - Security groups with appropriate rules on all access point interfaces
     - Typical configurations:
          - Within a VPC
          - From a customer VPC to an authorized service from the same IaaS/PaaS
          - From a customer VPC to an external authorized service from a different CSO
               - Can be allowed
               - Similar access rules must be applied from the access point to the external service

**Access Point Configuration Console (when required)**
- May or may not be required
     - A bastion host typically is configured locally
     - An identity proxy is often configured from a console located separately - sometimes even a cloud service
- Requirements
     - Inside an authorized boundary
     - Traffic from console to the access point is mutually authenticated
          - Sufficient example:
               - mTLS

**Internal Component**
- Any internal component lacking public accessibility
- Requirements:
     - User access must be uniquely identified
     - User authentication may be single factor when authenticated against the same user domain as used on the access point
          - Federation is sufficient to meet this requirement
          - For example, the following is sufficient:
               - CSO leverages an external, authorized identity management provider (IdP) CSO
               - External IdP users are federated to a user directory internal to the boundary
               - Admin authenticates to external IdP
               - External IdP presents authentication token to access point
               - Admin authenticates to internal component
               - Internal component leverages federated, internal directory to authenticate the admin
          - Note: It is insufficient for an admin to authenticate as a local user on an internal component without meeting the full AAL requirements on that device
     - Device to device authentication is sufficient when both:
          - Traffic from the access point to the internal interface is mutually authenticated and authorized at the device level
          - Unique user identity is presented and logged at the internal component

>

> 
> - Should be considered inside the authorization boundary, with no dual-purpose hosting of other functionality.
> 
> - All inbound traffic to the access point should be audited, authenticated, and authorized at the appropriate AAL.
> 
> - Logical separation should ensure that publicly accessible networks cannot traverse to internal components without explicit inspection/authorization.
>

> 
> - While the rescission of the Subnets White Paper provides flexibility beyond strict Layer 3 subnetting, updated guidance should continue to require deny-all / permit-by-exception rules at all access point interfaces.

> - In SDN-enabled environments, this means applying granular rules at every interface; in non-SDN environments (e.g., co-lo facilities), implementers should hew closer to traditional subnetting with demonstrable equivalent controls.

>

>

## Rene2mt — [2025-09-10T18:49:06Z](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/71#discussioncomment-14366427)

Bottom line up front (BLUF), the proposed change to **SC-7 (b) Additional FedRAMP Requirements and Guidance** is much needed, as it provides CSPs more modern and innovative options for satisfying the intent of SC-7(b), which IMO is ensuring systems establish strong network segmentation and isolation of (public-facing) resources.  SC-7(b) is very prescriptive when it states _"Implement subnetworks for publicly accessible system components that are [Selection: physically; logically] separated from internal organizational networks"_ however, this is grounded in an era that predates modern cloud computing and when DMZs were generally implemented via subnetting. We have lots of cloud-native tools nowadays that can be used (e.g., in combination) for implementing network segmentation and trust boundaries including VPCs, WAFs, ALB/ELBs, subnets, identity-based policies, security groups, network ACLs, IAP, ZTNA, and more. Subnet, while still very important, is one of many tools in the toolbox.

#### Recommendation 1

This is very minor, but consider updating  SC-7(b) updated guidance to "SC-7 (b) may be met by any **set of technical capabilities** that ensures..." as a way to emphasize that its not necessarily implementation of one technical capability that is required; there may be many cases where CSP implement several (cloud-native) capabilities that collectively provide the logical separation intended by SC-7(b).  

#### Recommendation 2

It may be worthwhile to establish a common FedRAMP definition for "publicly accessible" in the context of SC-7(b) to ensure that CSPs, 3PAOs, and federal agency customers are all aligned when assessing this control.  

I've seen some stakeholders interpret "publicly accessible" in a number of ways including:

- Directly accessible from the public internet (i.e., public IP, open port) without a mediating boundary (e.g., firewall, WAF, API gateway).
- Reachable from the internet without requiring authentication or identity enforcement.
- Even if residing in a private subnet, components that serve public traffic (via a public WAF, reverse proxy, or API gateway) are still functionally exposed and may meet the intent of "publicly accessible."

We need a common FedRAMP definition so that we're all on the same page.  Hopefully FedRAMP will establish a common definition that CSPs can demonstrate via (future) 20x KSIs.

## vedigaurav — [2025-09-11T14:11:49Z](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/71#discussioncomment-14374882)

1: Insufficient Guidance for Non-HTTP(S) Protocols

Gap Identified: Most discussions and examples have centered around web-based access methods (e.g., Google IAP, mTLS, identity proxies), yet many CSPs support operational workflows over non-web protocols such as SSH, RDP, SNMP, syslog, or database ports. These protocols pose unique challenges in meeting SC-7(b) without traditional inspection or proxy enforcement mechanisms.

Recommendation: FedRAMP should provide explicit examples and acceptable methods for enforcing logical separation, inspection, and authorization for non-HTTP(S) protocols. These examples should include guidance for SSH, RDP, SQL-based access, and agent-based communication, addressing how traversal control and access logging can be enforced when identity-aware proxying is not feasible.

2: Ambiguity Around the Terms "Inspection" and "Authorization"

Gap Identified: The updated guidance requires "preventing traversal without inspection and authorization" but does not clearly define either term, nor explain whether they must be co-located or sequential. This creates confusion for architectures where inspection and authorization are decoupled across different layers.

Recommendation: Clarify definitions of:

- Inspection: Does this mean deep packet inspection, metadata analysis, or logging?
- Authorization: Does this include identity-based rules, ACLs, or service-level RBAC?
Specify whether inspection and authorization must occur together at the same boundary control point or whether they can be distributed across layers, provided the traversal is still controlled and auditable.

3: No Consideration for Multi-Cloud or Hybrid Deployments

Gap Identified: The guidance assumes a single-cloud or homogenous environment. However, many CSPs operate multi-cloud platforms or hybrid-cloud infrastructures that include on-prem components or other federal networks. SC-7(b) implementation becomes more complex in these cases, especially for ensuring consistent boundary protections across providers.

Recommendation: Include guidance on how to implement and assess logical separation across cloud-to-cloud and cloud-to-on-prem boundaries. This should address:

- Cross-cloud identity enforcement
- Federated network control and inspection
- Minimum evidence or control parity expectations across environments

4: No Specific Treatment of Third-Party Administrator Access

Gap Identified: The updated guidance focuses on internal admin access points but does not address scenarios involving third-party administrators, such as Managed Service Providers (MSPs), contractors, or shared services (e.g., agency SOCs or FedRAMP-authorized security tools).

Recommendation: Define expectations for external administrative access, including:

- Required controls at the boundary between external providers and internal systems
- Identity federation, trust establishment, and logging requirements
- Whether third-party access must traverse an internal admin access point or can be direct with equivalent controls

5: Ambiguity Around SC-7(b) Applicability to Multi-Tenant SaaS

Gap Identified: The current update does not clarify whether SC-7(b) applies in multi-tenant SaaS or PaaS environments where boundaries are between tenants rather than public vs. internal. This leads to uncertainty about whether tenant isolation must meet the same standard as public-to-internal boundary separation.

Recommendation: Clarify whether SC-7(b) applies to inter-tenant boundaries in multi-tenant architectures. If so, provide guidance on:

- How logical separation and traversal control must be enforced between tenants
- Whether other controls (e.g., SC-4, AC-6, AC-3) are more appropriate for tenant boundary enforcement

## nicole-gov (FedRAMP) — [2025-09-12T14:12:05Z](https://github.com/FedRAMP/community/discussions/71#discussioncomment-14385114)

Public comment received via form from Ryan Gutwein reproduced in its entirety here:

---
While I support the effort to rebalance SC-7(b) between FedRAMP 20x and Rev5, I recommend the following clarifications and refinements for administrator access scenarios:

Scope of SC-7(b) – Customer vs. Administrator Access
SC-7(b) applies equally to customer-facing and administrator-facing components. Current guidance and much of the discussion has centered on admin access. I recommend FedRAMP explicitly call out that both use cases must be addressed, as the risks and technical implementations differ significantly.

Admin Access Points

Should be considered inside the authorization boundary, with no dual-purpose hosting of other functionality.

All inbound traffic to the access point should be audited, authenticated, and authorized at the appropriate AAL.

Logical separation should ensure that publicly accessible networks cannot traverse to internal components without explicit inspection/authorization.

Logical Separation Requirements

While the rescission of the Subnets White Paper provides flexibility beyond strict Layer 3 subnetting, updated guidance should continue to require deny-all / permit-by-exception rules at all access point interfaces.

In SDN-enabled environments, this means applying granular rules at every interface; in non-SDN environments (e.g., co-lo facilities), implementers should hew closer to traditional subnetting with demonstrable equivalent controls.

Downstream Authentication

Current allowances for single-factor authentication to downstream components (after MFA at the access point) should be revisited.

NIST SP 800-53 controls (IA-2(1), IA-8(2)) and EO 14028/CISA ZTA guidance emphasize MFA for privileged access to each networked resource.

I recommend FedRAMP clarify whether single-factor downstream is a deliberate policy relaxation, and if so, define the conditions under which it is acceptable (e.g., same user domain, auditable federated identity).

Mutual Authentication Language

The requirement for “mutual authentication” between admin access points and internal components is currently vague and risks excluding industry-standard solutions (e.g., AWS SSM, Azure Bastion, Google IAP, Zscaler).

I suggest refining this requirement to:

“The admin access point must individually authorize each request to internal resources. Authorization decisions should be enforced at the access point, preventing unauthorized traversal even where mutual authentication is not feasible.”

This maintains the intent of zero-trust enforcement while preserving flexibility across architectures.

Closing Thought
The Balance Improvement Release is a positive step toward harmonizing FedRAMP Rev5 with operational practices. The final guidance should provide flexibility for modern SDN-based and managed service architectures, while ensuring that SC-7(b) continues to drive strong separation, auditable access, and consistent enforcement of zero-trust principles.
